# Building Knowledge Graphs and Recommender Systems for Suggesting Reskilling and Upskilling Options from the Web

**Authors:** Albert Weichselbraun, Roger Waldvogel, Andreas Fraefel, Alexander van Schie, Philipp Kuntschik  
**Year:** 2022  
**Published in:** Information, Vol. 13, Issue 11 — MDPI (CareerCoach Project, Innosuisse)

---

## Problem Statement

Continuing education information is scattered across hundreds of different provider websites, each with its own structure, terminology, and level of completeness. A worker who needs to transition careers — whether because of automation, economic disruption, or personal ambition — cannot easily answer the question: "What courses should I take to move from my current role into a new one?"

No single, structured, queryable source of continuing education knowledge exists. There is no automated way to build such a resource from the open web, and no recommender system to turn raw course catalog data into actionable career and upskilling advice. This paper sets out to build both: a knowledge graph constructed from 488 education provider websites, and a recommender system that uses it to suggest career paths and the courses that bridge the gap.

## Key Contributions & Objectives

1. Adapts knowledge extraction methods (entity recognition, entity classification, entity linking, slot filling) to the continuing education and human resources domain.
2. Applies these methods across 488 education provider websites in the German-speaking Swiss labor market.
3. Constructs a continuing education knowledge graph with 73,969 nodes and 734,447 edges, serialized in RDF and queryable via SPARQL.
4. Develops a career path recommender that computes the relative skill gap between a user's current competencies and a target occupation, then minimizes it.
5. Develops a continuing education recommender that scores courses by a weighted combination of job-specific skill coverage (weighted by skill uniqueness) and total skill gap coverage.
6. Deploys the system in production for three companies for semantic search and data analytics.
7. Releases the CareerCoach 2022 gold standard evaluation dataset (169 documents, 3,800+ annotations) publicly on GitHub.

## Methodology / Theory / Framework

The pipeline has two major components. First, a knowledge graph construction pipeline that starts by segmenting HTML documents from course provider websites into meaningful units (page segments), classifying each segment by its semantic role (course title, prerequisites, learning objectives, etc.), extracting entities and linking them to an occupation knowledge base, and finally serializing everything into RDF with a custom ontology (Dublin Core + Schema.org + CareerCoach namespace).

Second, the recommender system: the career path recommender computes a relative skill gap as `|target_skills \ user_skills| / |target_skills|` and ranks occupations by how small this gap is. The continuing education recommender scores courses using a weighted combination of two factors — how specifically a course covers skills unique to a target job (using an IDF-style uniqueness weight), and how broadly it covers the user's total skill gap. A tunable parameter α lets users shift between these two priorities.

A fine-tuned DistilBERT model (German domain-adapted) handles entity recognition and classification, trained on a 28,000-document silver standard corpus that was automatically generated by the entity linking component.

## Software Tools / Setup Details

- distilbert-base-german-cased transformer, fine-tuned on silver and gold standard corpora
- Protege Ontop framework for transforming the proprietary ×28 occupation knowledge base into Linked Data
- RDF serialization (Dublin Core, Schema.org, SKOS, custom CareerCoach namespace)
- SPARQL interface for knowledge graph querying
- CareerCoach 2022 gold standard: https://github.com/fhgr/careercoach2022
- 488 Swiss and German-language education provider websites crawled and processed

## Test / Experiment Analysis

Six pipeline tasks evaluated (T1–T6) using precision, recall, and F1 on the CareerCoach 2022 gold standard (169 HTML documents from 89 providers). The recommender was evaluated by 3 domain experts with 18+ months of HR experience who ranked the top-15 occupation and course suggestions for 6 user profiles covering diverse educational backgrounds (no formal education, craft/trade, university). Evaluation metrics were Precision@3 and MAP@3 for career paths, and beneficialness and sufficiency at P@3 for continuing education courses. Inter-rater agreement measured with Fleiss' Kappa.

## Test Data / Dataset Source

- CareerCoach 2022 gold standard: 169 HTML documents, 3,800+ annotations (publicly available)
- Production corpus: 97,142 education course descriptions from 488 Swiss and German education providers
- ×28 occupation knowledge base: proprietary, 42 database tables, linked to SOC and industry directories

## Final Result

Pipeline results on the gold standard: page segmentation F1 = 0.83, entity recognition F1 = 0.73, slot filling F1 = 0.54–0.55. The full knowledge graph contains 73,969 nodes and 734,447 edges from 97,142 education descriptions.

Career path recommender: best case P@3 = 1.00 (office assistant); worst case P@3 = 0.33 (production employee). Continuing education recommender: best case Pb@3 = 1.00; expert inter-rater agreement Fleiss' Kappa = 0.42 (moderate for beneficialness), 0.19 (slight for sufficiency). Three commercial companies rated the data quality 4–5 out of 6 and deployed the system in production.

**What works well here:** This paper is the most structurally similar to CareerGraph's knowledge graph layer. The skill gap formula (`|target \ user| / |target|`) is essentially what our SkillGapAgent computes. The weighted course recommender balancing specificity and breadth maps closely to the tension CareerGraph's PathFinder navigates between depth of fit and coverage of missing skills. The production deployment is a strong signal that the approach is practically viable.

## Limitations

Slot filling F1 of ~0.54 means the system is not good enough for fully automated knowledge graph construction without human review — and the authors acknowledge this. The entity linking coverage had a significant gap: about 45% of relevant entities were not in the knowledge base and required the NER fallback. The continuing education coverage is biased toward formal institutions; trade-based and apprenticeship training is largely absent. The recommender does not account for course duration or effort cost, leading it to prefer comprehensive programs over shorter sufficient ones. Expert inter-rater agreement on sufficiency is very low (Kappa = 0.19), suggesting that even humans struggle to agree on what constitutes adequate coverage.

**The core limitation for career guidance:** The system models occupations and courses, but does not model individual users as first-class entities with persistent profiles. There is no "student node" in their knowledge graph — user profiles are computed on the fly and not persisted across sessions. This limits the ability to track progress, update skill levels, or generate personalized roadmaps.

## Final Summary

The CareerCoach system is the most direct published precedent for what CareerGraph is building. It proves the concept: a knowledge graph built from real-world sources, combined with a skill-gap-aware recommender, can produce actionable career guidance. Its production deployment by three companies validates the commercial viability of the approach.

**How CareerGraph does better:** CareerGraph represents students as first-class nodes in the Neo4j graph with persistent HAS_SKILL edges and proficiency weights — something CareerCoach never does. This means skill levels, proficiency scores, and career history are part of the graph and evolve over time. Our PathFinder also traverses explicit LEADS_TO skill prerequisite chains, which enables ordered learning paths that the CareerCoach recommender cannot produce — it can recommend courses but cannot tell you in what order to take them. Our NormalizationAgent handles the ~45% entity linking gap that plagued this system by combining a synonym map with rapidfuzz fuzzy matching against O*NET. And unlike CareerCoach's German-specific deployment, CareerGraph is designed with ESCO and O*NET integration for broader language and market coverage from day one.
